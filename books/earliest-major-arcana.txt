ngraved, first complete deck with 78 cards. At least one of the oldest numbered decks (perhaps the oldest of them). With the Boiardo poem and the "Sermones De Ludo Cum Aliis" it belongs also to the oldest sure evidence for the 22+4x14-structure of Tarot, so a precise dating of this deck is of some importance for the conclusions about the development of Tarot.

The motifs are Roman heroes, the cards contain inscriptions and numbers. A.M. Hind (1938) described a complete deck in the possession of the Sola Busca family, and that he was allowed to examine the cards in 1934, but the trace of this deck and the family is lost. Fragmented groups (copies of the same or a similar metalplate) of cards are in the possession of various other Museums. The productions are not totally identical.

Interestingly the trumps are numbered with the old Roman system, but the Matto card carries an Arabic "0". As we cannot date the invention of numbers on the cards precisely, the Sola Busca might be the oldest deck with numbers. The time of 15th century showed a revolution in the number system from Roman numbers to Arabic numbers, so the use of the Arabic "0" is of some importance.

The deck is with some probability dated to the year 1491, cause an inscription on the trump IV "Senatus Venetus" and on trump XIIII "Anno Ab Urbe Condito MLXX" which is interpreted as refering to a legendary date of the founding of Venice. However, not all versions of the Sola Busca show this inscription (see pictures to the right, the inscription is on the shields), so it might be, that the dated deck is the copy of an earlier. An alternative interpretation of the mentioned inscription suggests the year 1523.

Kaplan (Tarot Encyclopedia II, p. 297) points to a possible and earlier origin in Milan cause of a dedication "A FILIPPO" at the throne of the King of Coins and the use of the motto "Pax" on the banner of the same figure. Kaplan presents 23 pictures (from the Albertina Colllection, Vienna) at p. 126/127 Encyclopedia I and further 63 cards at p. 298-302, Encyclopedia II. This is not naturally a good idea, as another figure in the game is named Alexander (the great) and "Filippo" might refer to Alexanders father Philipp of Macedonia.

he claim that tarot was originally constructed around the 22 Hebrew letters assumes that the number and structure of the trumps were fixed from the beginning. The historical record points in a different direction.

In some of the earliest known tarot decks, particularly in the Visconti-Sforza context, the set of trumps is not identical to the later standardized sequence. These early forms can include additional theological virtues such as Faith, Hope, and Charity, alongside figures like Justice, Temperance, and Fortitude. This expanded set reflects a familiar Christian moral framework. It shows a system still in formation, drawing from the allegorical language of its time.

This matters because it indicates that the trump sequence was not originally a closed set of 22 elements. Instead, it was flexible, capable of expansion, and shaped by cultural context. A structure that can vary in this way does not point to a fixed esoteric code.

The case becomes even clearer when we look at Minchiate, a later Italian development. Minchiate expands the number of trumps significantly, often to around forty, adding not only virtues but also zodiac signs and other figures. This is not a marginal anomaly. It demonstrates that the idea of extending the trump series was entirely natural within the tradition.

Minchiate shows that the tarot framework could absorb additional symbolic material without losing coherence. That flexibility is difficult to reconcile with the idea of an original design based on a strict 22-part correspondence.

The number 22 becomes important only after the trump sequence stabilizes. Once a fixed set is in place, it becomes possible to align it with the 22 Hebrew letters and construct a coherent symbolic system. This is precisely what later authors, especially in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, begin to do.

The Kabbalistic reading is therefore best understood as a later layer. It is a structured reinterpretation applied to a set of images that had already taken shape within a different cultural framework.

A clear way to express this is:

Early tarot includes variable elements such as the theological virtues, and later developments like Minchiate expand the sequence even further. The fixed number of 22 belongs to a later stage, which makes the Kabbalistic correspondence a subsequent construction rather than an original design.

This perspective does not reduce the value of the Kabbalistic system. It clarifies its place in the history of tarot and allows each layer to be understood on its own terms.
